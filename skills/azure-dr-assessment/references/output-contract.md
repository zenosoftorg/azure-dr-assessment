# Output contract

Detail for stage 7 of `SKILL.md`. Everything goes below the output root, `dr-assessment/{app}/` in the working directory. The output root is the folder that holds `assessment.yaml`; `outputs.directory` in the file only records it, and a mismatch is a warning, never a different root. Announce the folder. Do not ask. Never write inside this skill's directory.

The one exception is the optional canvas copy of the diagrams, made only when the owner asks (stage 7): it goes to `architectures/` of the working folder, or to `$ARCH_CANVAS_DIAGRAM_DIR`, which is outside the output root and holds customer architecture. A team that commits the working folder should gitignore `architectures/`, or point `ARCH_CANVAS_DIAGRAM_DIR` at a folder inside the output root.

## The report
Sections 1 to 7 are the response structure of DR-TIER-001, in its order and with its names:
1. **Selected landing zone tier and recovery objectives.** The pattern, RPO, RTO and the cost profile as a planning ratio, never a price. Business targets, who set each, and any exception.
2. **Assumptions and failure scope.** Regions and their pair status, dependencies, exclusions, outstanding inputs.
3. **Proposed architecture.** The regional topology and the proposed diagram. Per component: its DR mechanism, class and status, any substitute, and the data-consistency approach. A class is given only to an Azure service's replication or recovery mechanism; a step that is a company pattern, a customer procedure or an on-premises action carries the pattern ID and version (or `customer procedure`) and no class: write "DNS cutover: DR-DNS-001 v1", never "DNS cutover: Any-pair".
4. **Failover and failback sequence.** Triggers, decision authority, order, estimated durations and verification, with the on-prem DNS step when DR-DNS-001 applies. A link to the runbook.
5. **RPO/RTO feasibility assessment.** Evidence, weakest link, risks and mitigations. Estimated or Demonstrated, and Unverified where proof is missing. If a target is not feasible, say so and offer a design change or a revised tier.
6. **Landing zone versus workload-team actions.** Platform-owned and workload-owned, with prerequisites. Unverified platform capabilities are dependencies.
7. **Validation plan and next steps.** Rehearsal scenarios, frequency, measurement, evidence, owners, the decisions still open and the implementation order. The Verification checks that were not run because an override waives their slot, each as `Not checked: waived by Override #<n>`.

Supporting sections follow, always in this order:
- **A. Current-state architecture.** Observed against inferred, with the diagram and presentation 1.
- **B. Evidence and unknowns.** What was read and what was not, every Inferred and Unknown fact, the region analysis. Generated by `report-tables`.
- **C. Documentation sources.** URL, retrieval date, Verified or Unverified, what it supported. Generated.
- **D. Pattern register and documented exceptions.** The pattern register, the decision per slot, the documented exceptions, the rejected overrides and the open inputs. Generated.

The report carries the banner right under its title and the DRAFT note when the pattern pack is not the company pack, and says that it is a draft for the owner's review.

## The establishment guide
It opens with the DRAFT line of the runbook. The target picture, the build order and the transition plan come first: build and validate beside today's paired DR, cut over, then decommission. Then one chapter per in-scope type, in build order (connectivity, identity, detection, secrets and keys, the data-replication slots and backup, compute, ingress, the traffic switch last), each with: Today (the current DR setup from the inventory, or Unknown, never absent), Target (the chosen option with its source, who chose it and why, or Default when nobody did), Prerequisites, Build, Validate (measurable test criteria), Cut-over, Decommission the paired setup (only after validation passed), Failover and failback (with a link to the runbook) and Open inputs. A chapter without a playbook says "No playbook, derived from Learn this run.". The consolidated test plan (service, criterion, pass condition, frequency, owner role) and a DR readiness checklist close it. `establishment-guide --check` passes on it. Every time is Estimated; nothing is called compliant, achieved or guaranteed.

## Concise and inline answers
If the user asks for a concise answer, or the run is inline (`SKILL.md`, Degraded modes), keep the same sections in the same order, with one table row or at most three bullets each. Keep the labels, the unknowns and the sources. An inline answer is not exempt from the labels: every replication or recovery mechanism it names, current or proposed, carries its class (Any-pair, Paired-only, No built-in multiregion) and its documentation status (Verified or Unverified), and every RPO or RTO figure is labelled Estimated, or Demonstrated only when a drill record exists (inline, there is none unless the user gives one). An inline answer has no evidence ids and no files: say what each fact rests on (the user's description or a Microsoft Learn page), and put the `NON-DEFAULT PATTERN PACK` banner first when the pattern pack is not the company pack.

## Per-recommendation trace
Each recommendation shows: its evidence ids, the documentation it rests on, the pattern ID and version if a pattern decided it (or the override index), and the decision or the open gap. A slot that an override waives without an alternative shows the source `override` and cites the Microsoft Learn documents it took its content from.

## The runbook
It opens with "DRAFT: not an operational procedure until rehearsed and approved by the owner." When the pattern pack is not the company pack, the pack banner follows as a blockquote line. It holds: preconditions and decision authority, detection and triggers, ordered recovery steps per service in section 3, one subsection per service in build order from its playbook (owner role, Estimated duration, verification, abort condition), a split-region service with its cross-region step, the traffic switch, business verification, failback and resynchronization, the return to steady state, and a test plan. Give commands or infrastructure-as-code only when asked, and label them untested.

## The presentations
- **Presentation 1, as-is.** Opens with a region mismatch when there is one, then the inferred links, the unknowns, the overview and one scene per group.
- **Presentation 2, the DR walkthrough.** Healthy state, establishing DR (one scene per build-order group: today's setup, the target and the test that proves it), disaster, detection, who approves, failover (including the DNS step), verification, failback, the RPO and RTO weakest link, and the documented exceptions when there are any, then the test plan.

## The chat summary
At the end, tell the owner in about fifteen lines: the tier and its objectives, the two regions and whether they are a pair, the headline gaps, the decisions the owner has to make, the documented exceptions, the DR options interview (the choices, and how many were defaulted, naming each defaulted type), the playbook folder when it is not the bundled one, and where the files are. The decisions and the interview choices are never cut.

## The pack banner
When the pattern pack is not the company pack, the `NON-DEFAULT PATTERN PACK` banner goes in five places: the title of each diagram, the cover blurb of each presentation, the report, the runbook and the chat summary.
