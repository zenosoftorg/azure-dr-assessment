# Azure Monitor Logs and Application Insights

## Mechanisms

### Workspace replication
- Replicates: data (new logs ingested after replication is enabled, asynchronously), table schemas and configuration changes, from the primary workspace to a secondary workspace that Azure Monitor creates in a supported secondary region in the same region group (see Constraints). Data that existed before replication was configured is not backfilled. It is a feature of the Log Analytics workspace, not of an Application Insights component
- Paired: yes
- Nonpaired: yes
- Failover: manual
- Estimated RPO: 2m (Learn: data is "typically replicated within about two minutes, but this timeframe isn't guaranteed"; logs or changes that are not yet replicated are unavailable in the secondary region; TC-2 measures the maximum unreplicated interval)
- Estimated RTO: unknown (Learn publishes no figure: the secondary region's ingestion endpoint "starts to receive data from agents a few minutes after you trigger switchover", the switchover activates ingestion in the secondary workspace and updates DNS records, "DNS propagation can take longer" than the record change, and clients that do not honor the DNS time-to-live can keep ingesting against the primary; the customer triggers it, so TC-3 measures it in a drill)
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/reliability-monitor-logs

### Data export
- Replicates: data (the logs of the workspace, continuously exported) to Azure Blob Storage; Learn says that if the region is paired, a geo-redundant storage (GRS) account replicates the exported logs asynchronously to the paired region. It is a backup copy of the data and not a second workspace: it has no switchover, and recovery is to deploy a new workspace and ingest recent data into it or to query the blobs with Azure Data Explorer
- Paired: yes
- Nonpaired: no
- Failover: manual
- Estimated RPO: unknown (Learn: the storage replication to the paired region is asynchronous and Learn publishes no figure; the export retry limit and the lack of protection against an ingestion pipeline incident are in the data export constraints below)
- Estimated RTO: unknown (Learn: after a disaster you deploy a new workspace and selectively ingest recent critical data, so the time depends on the volume and the team's runbook; Learn publishes no figure)
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/reliability-monitor-logs#custom-multiregion-solutions-for-resiliency

### Availability zones
- Replicates: data (log data replicated across availability zones with data resilience) and service operations (ingestion and queries continue with service resilience), inside one region only
- Paired: no
- Nonpaired: no
- Failover: Microsoft-managed
- Estimated RPO: unknown (Learn: for clusters that have data resilience "no data loss is expected for data that's fully committed", and agents or clients retry unacknowledged batches; Learn publishes no figure, and this protects against a zone failure only)
- Estimated RTO: unknown (Learn: for clusters that have service resilience "no downtime is expected", with temporary latency increase or throttling possible; no figure, and zone redundancy does not protect against the loss of a region)
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/reliability-monitor-logs#resilience-to-availability-zone-failures

### Application Insights over a Log Analytics workspace
- Replicates: nothing across regions. A workspace-based Application Insights component sends its telemetry to the workspace it is linked to, and Learn's workspace replication article lists "Application Insights over Log Analytics workspaces" as not supported for replication and switchover
- Paired: no
- Nonpaired: no
- Failover: not applicable
- Estimated RPO: unknown (no cross-region mechanism for the component; Learn publishes no figure)
- Estimated RTO: unknown (no cross-region mechanism for the component; recovery is a second component in another region, see dr-region-workspace)
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication#deployment-considerations

## Constraints

### Workspace replication needs a supported region pair in one region group
Learn: workspace replication "currently supports workspaces in a limited set of regions, organized by region groups"; the secondary region must be one of the supported secondary regions in the same region group as the primary (a workspace in West Europe can be replicated in North Europe, not in West US 2), and if the workspace's region is not listed it does not currently support replication. In Learn's table the Europe group lists, as secondary regions: France Central, Germany West Central, North Europe, South UK, West Europe and West UK; North America lists Canada Central, Central US, East US, East US 2, West US, West US 2 and West US 3. A region can be listed as a primary without being listed as a secondary (for example Sweden South appears among the Europe primary regions but not among the secondary regions), so the primary's pair is not always a valid secondary: check the DR region against the table in the run. Workspaces in East US, East US 2 and South Central US can only replicate to a secondary region outside that set of three.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication#supported-regions

### Switchover and switchback are manual and Microsoft does not trigger them
Learn: "There's no automatic failover. Switchover is a manual decision." Microsoft does not decide or start the process, "even if there's a region outage", and does not notify you automatically that a region is down (use Azure Service Health alerts). The customer must trigger the switchback as well. Because the customer triggers them, Learn says you can trigger a switchover and a switchback at any time, including to run tests or disaster recovery drills.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/reliability-monitor-logs

### Only new logs are replicated, and switchover has waiting periods
Replication copies only logs ingested after it is enabled; a workspace that enabled replication three hours ago can return only the last three hours from the secondary workspace. Learn: wait at least 24 hours after enabling replication, and confirm that the operation completed, before triggering a switchover ("Azure Monitor rejects earlier switchover requests"); Learn recommends waiting at least one week so that the secondary holds a useful volume of logs. After enabling, it can take up to one hour for all tables to begin replicating, schema changes can take up to one hour to start replicating, and disable requests are rejected during the first hour after enabling.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication

### Only data collection rules that are associated with the workspace endpoint replicate
Azure Monitor creates a system data collection endpoint (DCE) when replication is enabled. Data from Azure Monitor Agent, the Logs Ingestion API and Azure Event Hubs that arrives through data collection rules (DCRs) continues during a failover only for DCRs that are associated with that system DCE (its property `properties.metadata.provisionedByResourceId` identifies the workspace). DCRs that are not associated do not replicate their data. A DCR connected to the workspace DCE can target only that workspace: it must not target other workspaces or Azure Storage accounts.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication#associate-data-collection-rules-with-the-workspace-data-collection-endpoint

### Some features are not replicated
Learn: auxiliary-plan tables are not replicated (do not enable replication on workspaces that include them; their data is not available after switchover); "Application Insights over Log Analytics workspaces", VM Insights and Container Insights are listed as "Not supported"; search jobs and restore are partially supported (tables populated before replication are not replicated, and an operation in progress at switchover has an unexpected outcome); private links are supported. Microsoft Sentinel watchlist and threat intelligence tables refresh every 12 days, so it can take up to 12 days to fully replicate them. Log search alert rules are not replicated automatically; Learn says you can replicate them yourself (for example by exporting from the primary region and importing in the secondary).
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication#deployment-considerations

### Workspace management is blocked during switchover
During switchover, workspace management operations cannot be started: changes of retention, pricing tier and daily cap, changes of network settings, and schema changes (new custom logs, or platform logs from a new resource type). Learn adds that workspace management is always handled in the primary region, and that switchover is not likely to help for a cross-regional issue with an underlying resource or for a workspace management issue. A purge removes records from both workspaces and fails if one of them is unavailable, and a replicated workspace cannot be deleted until replication is disabled.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication

### Switchover reroutes ingestion with DNS
The switchover updates the DNS records of the ingestion endpoints to point to the secondary region, and the platform routes queries internally to the active region. Learn warns that DNS propagation can take longer than the record change and that clients must not cache DNS entries for longer than their time-to-live. Microsoft agents retry failed ingestion automatically, and applications that use the log ingestion API should also retry. After a switchover, if the primary region cannot process incoming data, Azure Monitor buffers it in the secondary region for up to 11 days, and during the first four days it automatically retries the replication periodically.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/reliability-monitor-logs#behavior-during-a-region-failure

### A dedicated cluster is replicated first, with the same secondary location
If the workspace is linked to a dedicated cluster, replication must be enabled on the cluster first and only then on the workspace, and both must use the same secondary location. Enabling it on the cluster creates a second cluster in the secondary region (Learn says no extra charge beyond replication charges) and can take 1-2 hours, and a cluster-managed key keeps working with the same key during failover. Changing the replication destination of a cluster requires disabling replication and enabling it again against another location, and removing cluster replication takes 14 days (Learn suggests an Azure support request if it must be quicker); the cluster can be disabled only after all linked workspaces have replication disabled.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication

### Changing the secondary location means disabling replication first
To change the location of the secondary workspace you must first disable replication, let the operation complete and only then enable replication to another secondary location.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication#what-to-check-if-workspace-replication-fails

### The roles that Learn names for switchover differ between its two statements
Learn's permissions table lists, for enabling replication, `Microsoft.OperationalInsights/workspaces/write` and `Microsoft.Insights/dataCollectionEndpoints/write`, and for switchover and switchback `Microsoft.OperationalInsights/locations/workspaces/failover`, `Microsoft.OperationalInsights/workspaces/failback`, `Microsoft.Insights/dataCollectionEndpoints/triggerFailover/action` and `Microsoft.Insights/dataCollectionEndpoints/triggerFailback/action`, "as provided by the Monitoring Contributor built-in role, for example". The same article says in its switchover troubleshooting that switching regions requires the Log Analytics Contributor role "on the resource group of the workspace, and not just on the workspace itself". The two statements are not worded the same; this playbook follows the stricter reading (see the Build step that grants both roles on the resource group of the workspace) and the drill of TC-3 proves it.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication

### Replication is billed on all ingested data
Learn's pricing model says that when replication is enabled you pay for the replication of all data you ingest to the workspace (except data with `_IsBillable` equal to false), and that if you do not associate your DCRs with the workspace endpoint "you're still charged for all the data you ingest to your workspace, even though the data isn't replicated". The section on associating DCRs says the association lets you specify the set of log streams to replicate, which "helps you control your replication costs". The two statements differ; this playbook follows the stricter one: budget for the charge on all ingested data, treat the DCR association as controlling what is replicated, and check the saving, if any, on the bill.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication

### Data export has limits and completeness rules
Data export rules export new data from selected tables. The destination must be in the same region as the workspace, a Premium storage account is not supported, the Auxiliary table plan is not supported, a workspace can have at most 10 active rules, custom logs made with the HTTP Data Collector API cannot be exported, and if the destination stays unavailable the retry process "continues for up to 12 hours" and the data is then discarded. Learn says to replicate exported data to other regions with the Azure Storage redundancy options, including GRS and GZRS.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/logs-data-export

### Data export is a backup copy, not a failover
Learn's best-practices article says the export mechanism "doesn't provide protection from incidents impacting the ingestion pipeline or the export process itself", and its comparison table marks data export with data backup only, not service resilience or high availability. Exported data sits in five-minute blobs, which is cumbersome to analyze in Azure Monitor Logs; Learn suggests Azure Data Explorer, Azure Data Factory or another storage access tool.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/best-practices-logs#reliability

### Azure Monitor Logs has no point-in-time backup or restore
Learn: Azure Monitor Logs "doesn't provide a traditional point-in-time backup or restore capability"; durability and recovery rely on intraregion replication (including zone redundancy), optional workspace replication, and data export to maintain an external copy.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/reliability-monitor-logs#backup-and-restore

### Dual write and export-and-rehydrate are Learn's custom multiregion solutions
Learn names two alternatives "if workspace replication isn't available for your region, or if you must use a table type that workspace replication doesn't support". Dual write configures sources (diagnostic settings, agents, DCR-based ingestion) to send logs to two independent workspaces in different regions; it gives near real-time analytics in both regions, doubles the ingestion cost and "introduces the risk of configuration drift between the workspaces". Export and rehydrate applies "if your region is paired with another Azure region": data export to a GRS storage account, then, during a disaster, a new workspace into which recent critical data is selectively ingested, with long-term history queried from Blob Storage with Azure Data Explorer.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/reliability-monitor-logs#custom-multiregion-solutions-for-resiliency

### A workspace-based Application Insights component keeps its data in its linked workspace
Application Insights resources that this playbook covers are workspace-based (Learn: classic resources have been retired). Data ingestion and retention are billed through the Log Analytics workspace where the data is located, and retention is set in the workspace. If you do not connect an existing workspace when you create the component, a new Log Analytics workspace is created automatically. A component can be linked to a workspace in another subscription only if the identity has the `Microsoft.OperationalInsights/workspaces/sharedKeys/action` permission on that workspace, otherwise the operation can fail with 403.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/app/create-workspace-resource

### Telemetry in an old Application Insights resource does not move to a new one
Learn: when application components are pointed at a new, consolidated Application Insights resource, "the telemetry stored in your old resource doesn't transfer to the new resource". Learn also says the legacy continuous export is not supported for workspace-based resources and points to diagnostic settings (archive to a storage account or stream to an event hub). So a second component in the DR region starts empty and holds only the telemetry that is sent to it after its creation.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/app/create-workspace-resource

### Learn recommends the component and its workspace in the same region
Learn's Application Insights guidance: deploy the Application Insights resource in the same region as the underlying Log Analytics workspace, because with the two in different regions "a regional failure" has twice the chance to cause problems; use connection strings, which "remove dependencies on global ingestion endpoints"; and adopt infrastructure as code (Bicep templates) to create or re-create the alerts, dashboards and queries of the component quickly after a service failure.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/service-guides/application-insights

### Availability zones protect against zone failures only
Learn: availability zones protect against infrastructure-related incidents such as storage failures and "don't protect against application-level issues, such as faulty code deployments or certificate failures, which impact the entire region"; they do not protect against issues that impact the whole region. Data resilience protects stored data, while service operations such as ingestion and queries continue only with service resilience, which only some regions offer.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/best-practices-logs#in-region-protection-using-availability-zones

## Non-paired options

### workspace-replication: Replicate the Log Analytics workspace to the DR region
- Kind: native
- Target: dr-region
- Uses mechanism: Workspace replication
- Estimated RPO: 2m (Learn: data is typically replicated within about two minutes, a typical figure that Learn does not commit to; new logs only, from the moment replication is enabled; TC-2 measures it)
- Estimated RTO: 15m (Estimated, team-set starting value; TC-3 proves it. Learn gives no figure, only that the secondary region's ingestion endpoint "starts to receive data from agents a few minutes after you trigger switchover" and that switchover "isn't instantaneous" because it relies on DNS updates that "some clients pick up within minutes while others can take more time", so the team value allows for slow DNS clients; the customer triggers the switchover — https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication)
- Prerequisites: a primary region that Learn lists as a primary, and a DR region that Learn lists as a secondary region in the same region group (East US, East US 2 and South Central US cannot replicate to one another); the residency check for the DR region, because Azure Monitor stores logs in the primary and the secondary region that you choose; no Auxiliary-plan tables in the workspace; if the workspace is linked to a dedicated cluster, replication enabled on the cluster first with the same secondary location, and a decision about the 14 days that removing cluster replication takes later; identity and RBAC: a switchover identity group that holds Log Analytics Contributor and Monitoring Contributor on the resource group of the workspace (a Contributor cannot assign them; an Owner or User Access Administrator does), and responders with Log Analytics Reader on the workspace to query it; networking and DNS: private links are supported by replication, and every agent and client must honor the DNS time-to-live of the ingestion endpoints and retry failed ingestion; every DCR that carries a critical stream associated with the system data collection endpoint and targeting only the workspace; the REST or `az rest` call for enabling replication (Learn enables it by a REST command), with the workspace's existing settings kept; query auditing enabled so that LAQueryLogs records `isWorkspaceInFailover`; the log search alert rules, dashboards and workbooks copied to the secondary region by infrastructure as code.
- Cost notes: replication charges for all data ingested to the workspace (Learn's texts differ on whether associating only the DCRs of critical streams with the workspace endpoint lowers the charge; plan for the stricter reading, see Constraints); a dedicated cluster adds a second cluster in the secondary region at no charge beyond replication charges (Learn); the monitoring queries and alert rules of the runbook; no price.
- Gives up: automatic failover (the customer decides and triggers the switchover and the switchback); support for a workspace-based Application Insights component, VM Insights and Container Insights, and for Auxiliary tables (Learn lists them as not supported, so they need dr-region-workspace or another design); the history before replication was enabled; workspace management during switchover; a free choice of DR region (only Learn's supported secondary regions of the region group); a view of the secondary workspace (it is not a separate manageable resource and is queried only through the workspace endpoint).
- Recommended when: the DR region is a supported secondary of the primary's region group (for example a DR region that is not the primary's pair), the workspace holds tables that replication supports, and the team can run a manual switchover with documented criteria.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-monitor/logs/workspace-replication

### dr-region-workspace: Second workspace and Application Insights component in the DR region
- Kind: workaround
- Target: dr-region
- Uses mechanism: none
- Estimated RPO: 5m (Estimated, team-set starting value; TC-4 proves it. Learn says dual write gives "near real-time analytics in both regions" and publishes no figure; the lag between the two workspaces is a property of each source, and the number is the lag bound that the team sets for the source types)
- Estimated RTO: 15m (Estimated, team-set starting value; TC-5 proves it. It is the switch-over target the team sets for pointing queries, alert rules, workbooks and the DR deployment at the DR workspace and the DR component, because Learn publishes no figure for a custom multiregion solution)
- Prerequisites: a Log Analytics workspace in the DR region, created from the same template as the primary (retention, plan, daily cap and tables) so that drift is visible; for every source type, a second destination: diagnostic settings, agents and DCR-based ingestion configured to send logs to both workspaces (Learn's dual write); for a workspace-based Application Insights component, a second component in the DR region linked to the DR workspace (create it with the existing-workspace link so that no workspace is created automatically, using `az extension add -n application-insights` and `az monitor app-insights component create` as Learn describes), and the DR deployment's own connection string in its configuration; if the DR workspace is in another subscription than its component, the `Microsoft.OperationalInsights/workspaces/sharedKeys/action` permission for the identity that links them; identity and RBAC: Log Analytics Reader on the DR workspace for the responders that query it, and a write role on the resource groups of both regions for the platform engineer who deploys the DR workspace, component and alert rules and enables the primary's alert rules again at failback (assigned by an Owner or User Access Administrator; a Contributor cannot assign roles); networking and DNS: the DR workspace and component reachable from the DR network by the same path as the primary's (a private link needs its own private endpoint and DNS zone link in the DR network, see the private-endpoints-dns playbook); infrastructure as code that creates the alerts, dashboards and queries in both regions.
- Cost notes: Learn says dual write "doubles the ingestion cost"; the DR component's ingestion and retention are billed through the DR workspace; the second set of alert rules and queries; no price.
- Gives up: automatic failover (consumers are pointed at the DR workspace by the team); history before the dual write started (inference: Learn names no mechanism that copies existing data into the second workspace); configuration parity (Learn: "the risk of configuration drift between the workspaces"); one place for the data (queries span two workspaces); the primary's component telemetry (the DR component starts empty and holds only telemetry sent to it; Learn says telemetry does not transfer between components).
- Recommended when: the DR region is not a supported secondary for workspace replication, the workspace holds tables replication does not support, or the data to protect is workspace-based Application Insights telemetry; and a doubled ingestion cost for the critical sources is acceptable.
- Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/reliability-monitor-logs#custom-multiregion-solutions-for-resiliency

## Migration from a paired setup

### Recognise today's setup
Learn describes no Microsoft-managed region failover for Azure Monitor Logs (workspace replication has "no automatic failover"), so today's paired DR shows as one of: (1) workspace replication to a region that is the pair (the workspace has `replication` settings with `enabled` and a secondary `location`; the portal Essentials section shows a Secondary location; a dedicated cluster shows the same); (2) data export rules (`az monitor log-analytics workspace data-export list`) to a GRS or GZRS storage account, whose pair holds the blobs; (3) availability zones only (nothing in another region); or (4) nothing: one workspace and one component per application. A workspace-based Application Insights component shows its linked workspace (Overview pane) and its connection string. The inventory cannot show whether each DCR is associated with the system DCE (read each DCR), whether alert rules and dashboards have a copy in another region, whether a client honors the DNS time-to-live, or whether the switchover was ever rehearsed; the secondary workspace is not a resource and does not appear. Migration is decided per workspace, by option id: workspace-replication when the DR region is a supported secondary of the primary's region group; dr-region-workspace for a DR region outside the list, for Application Insights components, and for tables replication does not support. Both can be mixed across one workload, never for the same data.

### Build
1. Take the inventory of the workload's log sources and classify each: workspace and its region and cluster link; tables and their plan (Auxiliary tables need a second workspace in the DR region); DCRs and the streams they carry; diagnostic settings, agents, Logs Ingestion API clients and Event Hubs sources; workspace-based Application Insights components and their linked workspaces; VM Insights and Container Insights. The result is the list that TC-6 compares.
2. workspace-replication: check the DR region against Learn's table of region groups (primary region listed as a primary, DR region listed as a secondary in the same region group, and the East US, East US 2 and South Central US rule) and do the residency check. When the DR region is not a listed secondary, record workspace-replication as not available for this workspace and use dr-region-workspace.
3. workspace-replication: on the resource group of the workspace, have an Owner or User Access Administrator assign Log Analytics Contributor and Monitoring Contributor to the switchover identity group (the role the Failover and Failback steps use, and the stricter reading of Learn), Monitoring Contributor to the same group on the resource group that holds the alert rule copies for the secondary region (the failover step that checks the alert rules in the secondary region; an inference, because Learn names no role for deploying alert rules), and Log Analytics Reader on the workspace to the responders. Contributor cannot assign roles. The platform engineer is a member of the switchover identity group.
4. workspace-replication: enable query auditing on the workspace so that LAQueryLogs records `isWorkspaceInFailover` and `workspaceRegion`; both are used to verify the switchover and the switchback.
5. workspace-replication: when today's workspace already replicates to the pair and the DR region differs, decide the gap: replication cannot go to two regions, so the existing replication has to be disabled (wait at least one hour after it was enabled; `az monitor log-analytics workspace update --replication-enabled false`), the operation must complete, and only then can replication be enabled to the DR region (Learn: the old copies are removed only when the feature is fully disabled). This means a window with no replicated copy; as an inference from those rules, cover it by keeping the data export rules to GRS and, for the critical sources, the dual write of dr-region-workspace until TC-2 passes for the new secondary.
6. workspace-replication: for a dedicated cluster that already replicates to the pair, after the replication of every linked workspace is disabled (the Build step that disables today's replication to the pair) disable the cluster's replication (`az monitor log-analytics cluster update --replication-enabled false`; Learn: it can be done only after disabling it for all linked workspaces, and changing the destination requires disabling and enabling again) and wait until the removal completes, which Learn says takes 14 days (an Azure support request if it must be quicker); the window with no replicated copy then lasts at least that long, so keep the covering measures of the Build step that disables today's replication to the pair until TC-2 passes. Then, for any dedicated cluster, enable replication on the cluster first, with the DR region as secondary location (the Clusters - Create Or Update REST call with `properties.replication.enabled` true and `properties.replication.location`, keeping the cluster's other settings, because a PUT replaces them); check `az monitor log-analytics cluster show --query "replication.provisioningState"` until it is Succeeded (a new cluster can take 1-2 hours).
7. workspace-replication: enable replication on the workspace with the Workspaces - Create Or Update REST call (`az rest --method put`), the workspace's own location as `location` and `properties.replication.enabled` true and `properties.replication.location` set to the DR region, keeping the workspace's other settings; check `az monitor log-analytics workspace show --query "replication.provisioningState"` until it changes from Updating to Succeeded and the portal Essentials section shows the DR region as Secondary location. Allow up to one hour for all tables to begin replicating.
8. workspace-replication: associate every DCR that carries a critical stream with the system DCE (portal: the DCR's Overview, Configure DCE) and check that the endpoint's `properties.metadata.provisionedByResourceId` equals the workspace resource id; split any DCR that also targets another workspace or a storage account, because a DCR on the workspace endpoint can target only that workspace.
9. workspace-replication: replicate what replication does not: copy the log search alert rules (and the workbooks and dashboards that matter in DR) to the secondary region by infrastructure as code; create the monitoring Learn recommends for the active region (replication health, ingestion latency, workspace health metrics, Service Health alerts) with alert rules that notify and do not switch over by themselves (Learn: the decision "shouldn't be done automatically"); write the switchover and switchback criteria in the runbook.
10. dr-region-workspace: create the DR workspace in the DR region from the primary's template (same retention, plan, daily cap, table plans and tags), then add it as a second destination of every source type of the inventory (diagnostic settings, agents, DCR-based ingestion, Logs Ingestion API clients), taking one source type at a time and recording each in the inventory of the Build step that inventories the workload's log sources.
11. dr-region-workspace: for every workspace-based Application Insights component, create a second component in the DR region and link it to the DR workspace, with the `az monitor app-insights component create` command of Learn after `az extension add -n application-insights`, passing the DR workspace so that no new workspace is created; read its connection string (`az monitor app-insights component show`, field `connectionString`) and put it in the DR deployment's own configuration (an app setting or environment variable set per region by the release pipeline), so that the DR deployment sends telemetry to the DR component from deployment time.
12. dr-region-workspace: have an Owner or User Access Administrator assign Log Analytics Reader on the DR workspace to the responders (the failover step that checks the dual-written data in the DR workspace) and a write role (for example Contributor on the resource group; an inference, because Learn names no role for these deployments) to the platform engineer on the resource group of the DR region (the failover step that points the consumers at the DR workspace) and on the resource group of the primary region's workload (the failback step that points the consumers back at the primary workspace); the application engineer changes only the deployment's configuration, with the permissions the release pipeline already has; recreate the alert rules, dashboards and queries of the component and the workspace in the DR region from infrastructure as code (Learn recommends Bicep templates for this).
13. Run the first full check: TC-1 and TC-2 for workspace-replication, TC-4 for dr-region-workspace, and TC-6 and TC-8 for both; keep the existing setup until Validate has passed.

### Validate
1. workspace-replication: run TC-1 weekly and after every change to the cluster, the DCRs or the workspace settings, and fix any difference before going on.
2. workspace-replication: run TC-2 daily for 7 consecutive days before cut-over, then weekly.
3. workspace-replication: wait at least 24 hours after enabling (Learn recommends at least one week), then run TC-3 as a drill in a nonproduction environment or in a low-risk window and record the Demonstrated RTO and the Demonstrated RPO.
4. dr-region-workspace: run TC-4 daily for 7 consecutive days before cut-over, then weekly.
5. dr-region-workspace: run TC-5 in every DR drill, with the DR deployment pointed at the DR component and the consumers at the DR workspace.
6. Run TC-6 monthly, and after every change to a log source, a DCR or a monitored workload; TC-7 quarterly and after every change to a DR role assignment; and TC-8 monthly and after every alert rule change.
7. Record each result with its date; a time or interval that a drill measured is Demonstrated, and every other figure stays Estimated or Unknown.

### Cut over
1. workspace-replication: put the Failover and Failback steps for workspace-replication in the DR runbook, with the switchover criteria, the owner who decides and the identity group that triggers; mark the workspace's DR as customer-triggered switchover to the secondary region (not Microsoft-managed).
2. dr-region-workspace: put the Failover and Failback steps for dr-region-workspace in the DR runbook; make the DR workspace and the DR component the targets of the DR deployment and of the DR consumers (queries, alert rules, workbooks).
3. Remove from the runbook the steps that rely on the pair-region copy that is now replaced (for example a rehydrate from the paired storage account) only after Validate has passed; keep the other steps.

### Decommission
1. workspace-replication: when replication used to go to a pair-region secondary and the new replication to the DR region is validated, the old replication is already gone (it had to be disabled in the Build step that disables today's replication to the pair); nothing more is removed from the workspace.
2. dr-region-workspace: when workspace replication to the pair was enabled and the option is dr-region-workspace, disable it (`az monitor log-analytics workspace update --replication-enabled false`, at least one hour after enabling) and let the operation complete; for a linked dedicated cluster disable the workspaces first and then the cluster (cluster removal takes 14 days).
3. Remove the alert rules, dashboards and DR runbook steps that point at the replaced paired setup, only after validation passed; leave the data export rules to GRS storage in place unless the team decides they are no longer a wanted backup.

### Keep until cut-over
Keep today's workspace replication to the pair (if any, and until the Build step that disables today's replication to the pair forces its disabling), the data export rules to the GRS or GZRS storage account, the existing diagnostic settings and agents, the primary workspace's alert rules and the runbook steps of today's recovery, until TC-1, TC-2 and TC-3 (workspace-replication) or TC-4 and TC-5 (dr-region-workspace) and TC-6 have passed. After cut-over, the primary workspace, the primary component and the export rules stay for good.

## Test criteria

### TC-1: Replication is enabled to the DR region and healthy
- Applies to: workspace-replication
- Pass condition: the workspace's replication provisioning state is Succeeded and its secondary location is the DR region; where the workspace is linked to a dedicated cluster, the cluster's replication provisioning state is Succeeded with the same secondary location; the count of Auxiliary-plan tables in the workspace is 0; and every DCR that carries a critical stream is associated with the system DCE whose `properties.metadata.provisionedByResourceId` equals the workspace resource id and targets no other destination.
- How to measure: `az monitor log-analytics workspace show --resource-group <group> --workspace-name <workspace> --query "replication.provisioningState"` and the same call for the replication location; `az monitor log-analytics cluster show --query "replication.provisioningState"` for a cluster; the table list of the workspace for the plan of each table; the DCR list with the endpoint each one is associated with.
- Evidence: the command outputs with their date, the table list with the plan of each table, and the DCR list with its associated endpoint and its destinations.
- Frequency: weekly and after every change to the cluster, the DCRs or the workspace settings

### TC-2: The replication lag stays within the bound the team sets
- Applies to: workspace-replication
- Pass condition: for 7 consecutive days the 95th percentile of the unreplicated interval, sampled every 5 minutes as the newest record of each replicated critical table in the primary region minus the newest record of the same table in the secondary region, stays at or below the lag bound that the team sets and records before the test, with the Estimated RPO of the option as its starting value (if the measured percentile is above it, the measured value is recorded and the Estimated RPO is revised, because Learn says the two minutes are not assured); and the record counts per table are equal for every complete hour that ended before that bound. The maximum is recorded as the Demonstrated RPO of the test and is not a pass condition on its own.
- How to measure: query the workspace with the Log Analytics query API twice per sample, once with `overrideWorkspaceRegion=primary` and once with `overrideWorkspaceRegion=secondary`, and compare the newest record and the hourly count per table; add a canary record per source type and time its appearance in the secondary. Learn says replication runs in batches and the measured latency "can fluctuate and doesn't indicate any health issue", so a single high reading does not fail the test and the percentile decides.
- Evidence: the 7-day export of the samples with the percentile and the maximum per table and the hourly count differences.
- Frequency: daily for 7 consecutive days before cut-over, then weekly

### TC-3: A switchover and a switchback complete, and the Demonstrated RTO is measured
- Applies to: workspace-replication
- Pass condition: in a drill that starts at least 24 hours after replication was enabled, the switchover completes with the provisioning state Succeeded and LAQueryLogs showing `isWorkspaceInFailover` true; a canary record of each source type sent after the trigger is queryable in the workspace; the measured time from the trigger to the first queryable canary record per source type is at or below the switchover target that the team sets and records before the drill, with the Estimated RTO of 15 minutes of the option as its starting value (the Demonstrated RTO); the same holds for the switchback, with `isWorkspaceInFailover` false; and the log search test alert fires in the secondary region.
- How to measure: run the failover steps from the DR declaration to the check of the alert rules in the secondary region, and the failback steps from the check that the primary region is healthy to the confirmation that ingestion lands in the primary, in a nonproduction environment, or in a low-risk window if in production, simulating the runbook criteria when possible (Learn: this tests detection and automation as well); time each step; measure the maximum unreplicated interval and compare sample records between the primary and secondary with the query API.
- Evidence: the drill record with its timestamps, the command outputs, the LAQueryLogs rows, the canary record times and the measured Demonstrated RTO and Demonstrated RPO.
- Frequency: every DR drill, and once before cut-over

### TC-4: The DR workspace receives the dual-written data and stays in parity
- Applies to: dr-region-workspace
- Pass condition: for 7 consecutive days every dual-written table has the same hourly record count in the primary and the DR workspace for every complete hour that ended before the lag bound that the team sets and records for the source type, with the Estimated RPO of 5 minutes of the option as its starting value; the ingestion latency (90th percentile, per table) of the DR workspace is not above the lag bound; and the DR workspace's retention, plan, daily cap and table plans equal the primary's (zero differences against the infrastructure-as-code template), because Learn warns of configuration drift between the two workspaces.
- How to measure: the same hourly count query against both workspaces, the Learn ingestion-latency query (`ingestion_time()-TimeGenerated`, 90th percentile) on both, a canary record per source type, and a comparison of the workspace settings with the template.
- Evidence: the 7-day export of the counts and latencies, the canary times and the drift report.
- Frequency: daily for 7 consecutive days before cut-over, then weekly

### TC-5: The consumers and the DR component work from the DR region
- Applies to: dr-region-workspace
- Pass condition: in a drill, the DR deployment sends telemetry to the DR Application Insights component with its own connection string and no code change, telemetry appears in the component and in its linked DR workspace; a test alert on the DR workspace fires and notifies the on-call role; the DR workbook returns data; and the time from the start of the failover steps that check the dual-written data and point the consumers and the DR deployment at the DR workspace and component to the first successful check of each is at or below the switch-over target the team sets (the 15m of the Estimated RTO).
- How to measure: run the failover steps that check the dual-written data and point the consumers and the DR deployment at the DR workspace and component, and the failback steps that point the consumers and the primary deployment back at the primary workspace and component, in the drill; time them; check a telemetry count in the DR component and in the DR workspace (the `requests` or `traces` table of the component, the AppRequests or AppTraces table of the DR workspace, as the application uses), and fire a test alert rule.
- Evidence: the drill record with timestamps, the telemetry counts, the alert notification and the measured time.
- Frequency: every DR drill

### TC-6: Every log source has a DR path
- Applies to: workspace-replication, dr-region-workspace
- Pass condition: design check, not drillable as a whole. The inventory of log sources has 0 sources without a DR path: each is replicated through a DCR associated with the workspace endpoint, or sent to the DR workspace by the dual write, or its exception is recorded with the owner who accepted it; no workspace-based Application Insights component, VM Insights, Container Insights or Auxiliary table relies on workspace-replication alone (Learn lists them as not supported); and every component in the DR region is linked to a workspace in the same region.
- How to measure: take the inventory of workspaces, tables and their plans, DCRs and their endpoint association, diagnostic settings, agents, components and their linked workspaces, and compare them with the classification of the Build step that inventories the workload's log sources; check the second destination of each dual-written source type.
- Evidence: the inventory with the DR path of each source, dated, with the list of accepted exceptions and their owners.
- Frequency: monthly, and after every change to a log source, a DCR or a monitored workload

### TC-7: The roles for DR are in place on the right scopes
- Applies to: workspace-replication, dr-region-workspace
- Pass condition: design check. For workspace-replication the switchover identity group holds Log Analytics Contributor and Monitoring Contributor on the resource group of the workspace (and not only on the workspace) and the responders hold Log Analytics Reader on the workspace; for dr-region-workspace the responders hold Log Analytics Reader on the DR workspace, and the platform engineer's write role is assigned on the resource group of the DR region (the failover step that points the consumers at the DR workspace) and on the resource group of the primary region's workload (the failback step that points the consumers back at the primary workspace); no one holds the roles by an inherited default that the team did not record. TC-3 proves that the roles are sufficient for the switchover itself.
- How to measure: list the role assignments on the resource group and the workspaces (`az role assignment list --scope <scope>`) and compare them with the roles of the Failover and Failback steps.
- Evidence: the role assignment lists with their date and the comparison with the steps.
- Frequency: quarterly, and after every change to a DR role assignment

### TC-8: Alert rules, dashboards and workbooks exist in the other region
- Applies to: workspace-replication, dr-region-workspace
- Pass condition: every log search alert rule, dashboard and workbook that the on-call role needs in DR has an enabled copy in the DR region (Learn: alert rules are not replicated automatically with workspace replication), the copy is identical to the infrastructure-as-code definition, and a test alert on the copy fires and notifies the on-call role.
- How to measure: list the alert rules and workbooks of each region and compare them with the definitions in source control; fire a test alert in the secondary or DR region.
- Evidence: the two lists with their date, the diff against the definitions, and the alert notification.
- Frequency: monthly and after every alert rule change
